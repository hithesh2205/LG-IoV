--- Slide 3 ---
Problem Statement
Challenges in Internet of Vehicles (IoV)
Increasing Cyber-Attacks on Connected Vehicles: Modern vehicles are vulnerable to in-vehicle network (IVN) exploits (e.g., DoS, Fuzzy, RPM, and Gear spoofing attacks) and V2X wireless threats.
Privacy Concerns in Sharing Vehicle Data: Strict data privacy regulations (e.g., GDPR, CCPA) prevent vehicles from uploading raw telemetry, location history, or sensor logs to centralized servers.
Limitations of Centralized IDS:
Bandwidth bottlenecks from transmitting massive, continuous streams of high-frequency vehicular data.
Single point of failure: vulnerability to connection drops in high-mobility environments.


--- Slide 4 ---
 
Proposed Solution: FedIoV
Federated Learning (FL) Framework: Enables collaborative training where vehicles train intrusion detection models locally and only share encrypted model updates (gradients) with the server.
Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KANConvNet): A lightweight, edge-optimized classifier with learnable activation functions on edges rather than nodes, reducing computational overhead.
Layer-Wise Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE): Selectively encrypts only the most sensitive layers of the KAN model during aggregation, resolving the speed limitations of traditional FHE while preserving privacy.


--- Slide 5 ---
Project Objectives
Design a Collaborative IDS for IoV: Develop a distributed, cooperative system to detect cyber threats across the fleet without requiring a central database of raw logs.
Preserve Privacy of Vehicle Data: Leverage secure cryptographic channels and Layer-Wise FHE to ensure vehicle telemetry remains private.
Support Distributed Learning Across Vehicles: Build a robust Federated Learning client-server architecture capable of handling vehicle churn, communication drops, and non-IID settings.
Detect Vehicular Cyber Attacks Efficiently: Implement and optimize KANConvNet to detect low-latency attacks (target latency <60<60 ms) on resource-constrained Electronic Control Units (ECUs).

Threat Model
Step 1 – Set Objectives
Primary Objective: Build a real-time, privacy-preserving Intrusion Detection System (IDS) to secure the Internet of Vehicles (IoV) against cyber-threats.
Security Goals (CIA-P Triad):
Confidentiality: Protect vehicle network traffic and machine learning model weights from eavesdroppers.
Integrity: Ensure model updates are not tampered with, and prevent compromised vehicles from injecting malicious updates.
Availability: Ensure the system can detect and respond to attacks in real-time (under 60ms).
Privacy: Guarantee raw vehicle telemetry (GPS location, speed, routes) never leaves the on-board unit.


--- Slide 7 ---

Step 2 – Visualize
The Architecture (FedIoV / DCANFL):
Edge Client (Vehicle): Collects local CAN bus traffic →→ extracts a 46-dimensional "timeless" feature vector →→ trains a local KANConvNet model.

Secure Communication Channel: Vehicle handshakes with the Edge Server using Ed25519 (authentication) and ECDH-P256 (key exchange) to encrypt updates with AES-256-GCM.

Edge Server (Aggregator): Collects encrypted updates →→ runs Multi-Krum to filter outliers →→ averages updates →→ broadcasts the new global model.

Layer-Wise FHE (Fully Homomorphic Encryption): Selectively encrypts the most sensitive layer weights of the KAN ConvNet, allowing the server to aggregate them without decrypting them.



--- Slide 8 ---

Step 3 – Identify Threats
Threat 1: In-Vehicle Network Attacks (CAN Bus Exploitation)
Denial of Service (DoS): Attackers flood the CAN bus with high-priority messages, freezing brakes or steering controls.
Fuzzy / Impersonation Attacks: Attackers inject random or spoofed packets to confuse onboard ECUs.
Threat 2: Byzantine / Poisoning Attacks (Machine Learning Level)
Model Poisoning: Hacked vehicles upload corrupt model weights during federated training to blind the global model to specific attacks.
Threat 3: Communication Eavesdropping & Privacy Leakage
Man-in-the-Middle (MitM): Intercepting and modifying model updates in transit.
Reconstruction Attacks: Eavesdroppers reverse-engineering plaintext model weights to reconstruct raw driving telemetry



--- Slide 9 ---

Step 4 – Mitigate
Mitigating In-Vehicle Attacks:
KANConvNet IDS: Runs locally on the vehicle, analyzing overlapping sliding windows (W=64W=64, S=16S=16) to flag anomalous entropy, payload patterns, or ID frequencies.
Mitigating Federated Poisoning (Byzantine Attacks):
Multi-Krum & TOPSIS: The edge server calculates distance metrics between updates. Outliers (e.g., poisoned updates from hacked cars) are isolated and discarded.
Mitigating Network & Privacy Threats:
ECDH + AES-256-GCM: Establishes ephemeral key encryption for all transmissions (perfect forward secrecy).
Layer-Wise FHE: Fully Homomorphic Encryption is applied only to the most sensitive layers of the KANConvNet, keeping performance fast while guaranteeing cryptographic privacy.

--- Slide 12 ---
 
Justification
Coverage:
Low-Level IVN Protection: Car-Hacking and IEEE VTC-CAN evaluate anomaly detection on the internal physical ECU bus (message intervals, ID distributions, payload integrity).
High-Level V2V Protection: VeReMi evaluates system resilience to geographic and kinematic coordinate spoofing or communication misbehavior.
High-Level V2I/V2N Protection: CICIDS 2017 evaluates broad network-layer threats (DDoS, brute-force exploits, scans) targeting external connectivity.
Realism & Data Heterogeneity: Incorporating these four distinct datasets tests the dataset-agnostic feature engineering layer and the robustness of KANConvNet across high-frequency binary sequences, continuous float vectors, and IP traffic metrics.


--- Slide 13 ---
Data Engineering
Challenge: Heterogeneous DatasetsVarying Protocols: CAN bus messages (HEX payloads) vs. V2X beacons (coordinate floats) vs. IP flows (network counts).

Goal: Standardize diverse inputs into a single format so the same neural network (KANConvNet) can be reused.

Solution: Unified Feature Extraction
Sliding Window: Group incoming message streams into a sliding window of size W=64 messages and stride S=16.

Feature Unification: Summarize each window into exactly 46 universal features (numbers).

Unified Pipeline: Creates a dataset-agnostic feature vector x∈R^46 for the classifier model.

--- Slide 16 ---
Data Engineering (Preprocessing)
Data Cleaning Pipeline
Percentile Clipping: Continuous features are clipped at the 0.5th and 99.5th percentiles (using training set bounds) to remove noisy sensor spikes and outliers.
Z-score Standardization: Every feature is scaled to standard bounds.
Safeguarding Against Data Leakage (Anti-Cheating)
Centralized Stratified Split: 70% Train, 15% Validation, 15% Test.
Strict Training Isolation: Mean (μ) and standard deviation (σ) parameters are computed only on the 70% training split.
Evaluation Integrity: The calculated training statistics are locked and then used to scale validation and testing sets

--- Slide 18 ---
Non-IID Simulation
2.Dirichlet Non-IID Partitioning 

To model realistic geographic variations where different vehicles encounter different traffic patterns and attack ratios:

Mathematical Split: 
Training data is split across clients using a Dirichlet distribution with concentration parameter α=0.3.pc ∼ Dir(α⋅1N) where pc represents the proportion of samples from class c allocated to each of the N clients

Class Skew Effect: 

A smaller α leads to higher skew (non-IID conditions). 

With α=0.3, some vehicles observe only benign traffic, while others are dominated by a single attack class (e.g., DoS only), forcing the global model to learn from highly heterogeneous client updates.

Centralized Evaluation: 

Validation and test splits are kept centralized at the server, ensuring unbiased and global performance metrics.

--- Slide 26 ---

FINDINGS:
Fully HE Is Impractical in Our Scenario
 Latency: Real-time IDS requires 10–100 ms response times, but FHE delays processing to seconds or minutes. 
Hardware Constraints: Resource-heavy FHE drains batteries and starves safety applications on low-power On-Board Units.
Ciphertext Size: FHE expands model weights into gigabytes of data, saturating limited V2X wireless networks. 
Mobility: Slow FHE aggregation means fast-moving vehicles will likely drive out of network range before updates complete.
 Algorithmic Limits: Approximating essential non-linear IDS functions (like ReLU) with FHE triggers unsustainable computational overhead.

--- Slide 27 ---
Layered FHE
1. Full FHE encrypts all model parameters, making it too slow (seconds to minutes) and impractical for real-time IoV detection.
2. Layer-Wise FHE encrypts only the most sensitive layer of the KANConvNet model that captures vehicle-specific driving patterns.
3. All other layers (input, Fourier, activation, output) remain in plaintext to maintain speed and efficiency.
4. This approach maintains <60ms detection latency, keeps updates to kilobyte size, and runs on standard ECU-class hardware.
5. Key takeaway: Strong cryptographic privacy for critical vehicle data without sacrificing real-time performance.
